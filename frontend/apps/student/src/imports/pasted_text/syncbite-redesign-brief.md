Redesign and refine the existing SyncBite student-facing website.

IMPORTANT: Preserve the existing core functionality and current product structure. Do not add unnecessary features.

SyncBite is a campus meal prebooking platform initially designed for VIT Vellore students.

The website currently supports only two primary actions:

1. ORDER NOW
2. SCHEDULE ORDER

Students can browse campus restaurants and food stalls, select food items, choose a pickup time, pay online, and collect their food without standing in queues.

The goal of this redesign is to make SyncBite feel visually outstanding, energetic, funky, memorable, and alive while remaining extremely fast and effortless to use on mobile devices.

---

# 1. CORE PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY

The website should feel like a digital campus food festival.

It should NOT look like:

- A generic food delivery app
- A conventional SaaS dashboard
- A Swiggy/Zomato clone
- An overly minimal ecommerce website

It should feel like a product with its own personality.

The experience should communicate:

"Something exciting is happening here."

Students should enjoy opening and browsing the website, but ordering food must remain extremely simple.

Visual creativity must never interfere with usability or speed.

---

# 2. DESIGN DIRECTION

Transform the existing UI into a funky neo-brutalist editorial food marketplace.

Combine:

- Neo-brutalist web design
- Editorial food magazine aesthetics
- Street-food poster design
- Bold typography
- High contrast color blocks
- Playful hand-drawn annotations
- Asymmetric layouts
- Dynamic restaurant cards
- Lightweight animations
- Youthful campus energy

The design should feel intentional and unconventional.

Avoid making every component perfectly symmetrical and rounded.

Introduce controlled visual tension through typography, spacing, colors, and composition.

Do not make the website messy or difficult to use.

Target balance:

60% usability
25% funky editorial design
15% neo-brutalist energy

---

# 3. BRAND IDENTITY

Use the existing SyncBite logo and brand identity.

Retain the established visual language:

- Deep navy
- Green hero accents
- Warm cream/off-white backgrounds
- Raspberry pink
- Golden yellow
- Peach/orange highlights
- Rich food photography
- Hand-drawn illustrations and annotations

The existing green hero section is visually strong and should remain the foundation of the landing page.

Improve and expand the visual language around it rather than replacing its identity.

---

# 4. MOBILE-FIRST EXPERIENCE

Most students will access SyncBite using mobile phones.

Design mobile first.

Primary viewport:
390 × 844

Also support:
360 × 800
412 × 915

Desktop:
1440 × 900

Do not simply shrink the desktop design for mobile.

Create a deliberate mobile experience optimized for thumb interaction.

The first screen should immediately feel exciting and recognizable.

The website should feel fast enough to use during a short college break.

---

# 5. HOMEPAGE STRUCTURE

Keep the homepage concise.

Recommended order:

1. Navbar
2. Hero Section
3. Order Now / Schedule Order CTAs
4. Pick Your Food Spot
5. Featured Restaurant Cards
6. Popular Food Items (optional existing section only)
7. Simple Schedule Order CTA
8. Footer

Do not add new product features such as:

- Delivery
- Budget Mode
- Surprise Me
- Loyalty programs
- Reviews
- Complex recommendations
- Deals
- Extra dashboards

Keep the product focused.

---

# 6. HERO SECTION

Keep the existing green hero visual direction.

Make the mobile hero immersive and visually striking.

Use:

- SyncBite logo
- Large expressive headline
- Food-focused visual
- Deep navy background
- Green accents
- Playful editorial annotations

Recommended headline:

"YOUR FAVOURITE FOOD,
WITHOUT THE QUEUE."

Supporting text:

"Campus food, made easier. Pick your stall, choose your time, and collect without the wait."

The hero should feel cinematic but lightweight.

---

# 7. HERO AMBIENT ANIMATIONS

Use subtle ambient motion:

- Steam gently moving
- Small food elements floating slightly
- Slow camera parallax
- Tiny decorative movement
- Gentle visual breathing in the background

Avoid heavy video backgrounds that slow down mobile loading.

Avoid excessive scroll-triggered animations.

Animations should feel smooth and lightweight.

---

# 8. PRIMARY CTA DESIGN

The two primary actions must remain visually dominant.

## ORDER NOW

Use:

- Raspberry pink primary color
- Lightning icon
- Bold uppercase typography

Text:
"ORDER NOW →"

Description:
"Get food for the nearest available pickup time."

Make the button tactile with subtle hover and tap feedback.

---

## SCHEDULE ORDER

Use:

- Contrasting yellow, peach, or outlined design
- Clock/calendar icon
- Strong typography

Text:
"SCHEDULE ORDER →"

Description:
"Choose a future pickup time and collect later."

The two CTAs should feel visually different.

Order Now should feel energetic.

Schedule Order should feel intentional and premium.

---

# 9. DYNAMIC BACKGROUND COLOR SYSTEM

Do not keep the entire homepage on one background color.

Divide the page into visual chapters.

Recommended background journey:

HERO:
Deep Navy + Green

RESTAURANT DISCOVERY:
Warm Cream

POPULAR FOOD:
Raspberry Pink / Soft Red

SCHEDULE ORDER:
Peach / Butter Yellow

FOOTER:
Deep Navy

Use smooth transitions between sections.

The color changes should make scrolling feel exciting.

Do not use aggressive gradients everywhere.

Use strong blocks of color and controlled transitions.

The website should feel like the student is moving through different moods of a campus food marketplace.

---

# 10. MOBILE SCROLL EXPERIENCE

Scrolling should feel rewarding.

As the user scrolls:

- Background colors transition smoothly
- Food imagery enters naturally
- Decorative elements move subtly
- Restaurant cards respond with lightweight motion
- Typography reveals progressively

Avoid excessive scroll animations.

Do not make every element fly into the screen.

The website should feel alive without feeling slow.

---

# 11. RESTAURANT DISCOVERY SECTION

This is one of the most important parts of the redesign.

The current restaurant cards should be transformed from static generic tiles into visually distinctive food marketplace posters.

Section heading:

"PICK YOUR FOOD SPOT"

Supporting text:

"Your favourite campus stops, all in one place."

---

# 12. RESTAURANT CARD REDESIGN

Do not use a boring uniform grid as the primary mobile experience.

Use large horizontal swipeable restaurant cards.

Each card should occupy approximately 85–90% of the mobile screen width.

The student should focus on one restaurant at a time.

Restaurant cards should feel alive, tactile, and visually distinctive.

Use:

- Large high-quality food photography
- Properly visible food
- Strong restaurant typography
- Colored information strips
- Bold Open Now indicators
- Editorial labels
- Hand-drawn arrows or annotations
- Slightly varied card compositions

Do not crop food images excessively.

The food must remain recognizable and appetizing.

---

# 13. RESTAURANT CARD STRUCTURE

Each card should contain:

Restaurant image
Restaurant name
Food category
Open/Closed status
Estimated preparation time
Campus location
View Menu CTA

Example:

CAMPUS CAFE

South Indian · Beverages

● OPEN NOW

10–15 MIN

VIEW MENU →

Use strong typography and visual hierarchy.

Avoid tiny text and overly compact information.

---

# 14. CARD VISUAL VARIATION

Do not make every restaurant card identical.

Maintain consistency while introducing subtle variations.

Example:

Campus Cafe:
Green accent

Food Court:
Orange accent

North Indian Kitchen:
Raspberry accent

Juice Point:
Yellow accent

Use colored blocks, typography treatments, and editorial labels.

The restaurant marketplace should feel curated rather than database-generated.

---

# 15. RESTAURANT CARD INTERACTIONS

On hover or tap:

- Food image gently zooms 1.03x
- Card shifts upward slightly
- Arrow moves forward
- Status indicator subtly animates
- Information strip changes accent color

Keep interactions fast.

Animation duration:
150–300ms.

Do not use slow transitions.

---

# 16. MOBILE RESTAURANT CAROUSEL

On mobile:

Use horizontal scrolling restaurant cards.

Make swipe interaction obvious but subtle.

Example:

PICK YOUR FOOD SPOT

[ LARGE RESTAURANT CARD ]

← Swipe for more →

Do not force students to scroll through long vertical lists before seeing restaurants.

Prioritize immediate discovery.

---

# 17. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography should become a major part of the brand.

Use two typography styles:

DISPLAY:
Bold editorial serif or expressive condensed display font.

SUPPORTING:
Modern clean sans-serif.

Use oversized typography strategically.

Examples:

"YOUR FAVOURITE FOOD"

Large.

"WITHOUT THE QUEUE."

Even larger and highlighted.

Avoid making every text element look identical.

Typography should create personality.

---

# 18. HAND-DRAWN EDITORIAL ELEMENTS

Introduce small playful annotations inspired by food posters and editorial magazines.

Examples:

"Good food ahead →"

"Made for campus life"

"Fresh from the kitchen"

"Worth the walk"

"Skip the queue"

Use these sparingly.

They should support the brand and not interfere with navigation.

---

# 19. SIGNATURE SYNCBITE INTERACTION

Create a recognizable interaction when adding food to cart.

When a student taps ADD:

- Add button transforms instantly into quantity selector
- Cart count updates immediately
- Tiny food-themed visual travels subtly toward the cart
- Cart icon briefly reacts with a bounce or small movement

The interaction should be fast and satisfying.

Do not use heavy animations.

---

# 20. CART EXPERIENCE

Keep the cart compact and accessible.

On mobile:

Use a sticky bottom cart bar.

Example:

"2 Items · ₹110"

VIEW CART →

Do not let the cart occupy excessive screen space.

On desktop:

Use a compact sticky right-side cart panel.

The cart should remain functional and minimal.

---

# 21. SCHEDULE ORDER EXPERIENCE

Schedule Order should have a slightly different visual personality from Order Now.

ORDER NOW:
Energetic, immediate, raspberry/green accents.

SCHEDULE ORDER:
Calm, intentional, yellow/peach accents.

Use a simple date and time selector.

Example:

Today
Tomorrow
Wednesday

Available pickup slots:

12:30 PM
12:45 PM
1:00 PM
1:15 PM

Unavailable slots should be clearly disabled.

Do not create a complicated calendar interface.

---

# 22. HUMOROUS MICRO-INTERACTION SYSTEM

Introduce a lightweight personality system.

The website should occasionally react to unusual but harmless user behavior.

The humor should feel witty, self-aware, relatable to college students, and integrated into the SyncBite brand.

Do not use random jokes everywhere.

Use cooldowns and randomness.

Maximum 2–3 humorous interactions per browsing session.

Never interrupt payment, checkout, or important order actions.

Use toast notifications, floating stickers, subtle annotations, or lightweight popups.

Do not use browser alerts.

---

# 23. IMPLEMENT THESE 12 HUMOROUS INTERACTIONS

## 1. Excessive Downward Scrolling

Trigger:
User scrolls continuously for a long distance.

Message:
"Don't tear me down, bro. There's food up here too."

Visual:
Small toast with subtle shake animation.

---

## 2. Rapid Up-and-Down Scrolling

Trigger:
User scrolls rapidly up and down repeatedly.

Message:
"Are we exploring or having an existential crisis?"

Visual:
Small floating sticker.

---

## 3. Homepage Inactivity

Trigger:
User remains inactive on homepage for approximately 30 seconds.

Message:
"The food isn't going to choose itself."

Visual:
Bottom snackbar.

---

## 4. Repeated Back-and-Forward Navigation

Trigger:
User repeatedly navigates back and forward.

Message:
"Commitment issues detected."

Visual:
Small popup near navigation.

---

## 5. Repeatedly Opening the Same Restaurant

Trigger:
User visits the same restaurant multiple times in a short period.

Message:
"You've visited this stall more than your actual classes."

Visual:
Small annotation near restaurant card.

---

## 6. Long Browsing Without Ordering

Trigger:
User browses for an extended period without adding food.

Message:
"At this point, you're not hungry. You're conducting research."

Visual:
Temporary toast.

---

## 7. Adding and Removing the Same Food Repeatedly

Trigger:
User repeatedly adds and removes the same item.

Message:
"Are we ordering or negotiating?"

Visual:
Small cart animation.

---

## 8. Opening Empty Cart Repeatedly

Trigger:
User repeatedly opens an empty cart.

Message:
"This cart has trust issues."

Visual:
Cart-area message.

---

## 9. Adding an Unusually Large Quantity

Trigger:
User adds an unusually large quantity of food items.

Message:
"Planning a feast or feeding the entire hostel?"

Visual:
Floating notification.

---

## 10. Returning After Prolonged Inactivity

Trigger:
User returns to the website after being inactive for a significant period.

Message:
"Welcome back. We saved your seat."

Visual:
Small welcome toast.

---

## 11. Successful Order Completion

Trigger:
Payment and order successfully completed.

Message:
"Order locked in. Your future self will thank you."

Visual:
Subtle celebration animation.

---

## 12. Reaching the Bottom of the Homepage

Trigger:
User reaches the bottom of the homepage.

Message:
"Congratulations. You found the bottom of the internet."

Visual:
Small footer animation.

---

# 24. HUMOR IMPLEMENTATION RULES

Important:

- Do not show jokes repeatedly.
- Use session-based cooldowns.
- Same interaction should not repeat within a short period.
- Maximum 2–3 humorous interactions per session.
- Messages should disappear automatically after 3–4 seconds.
- Never block the screen.
- Never interrupt checkout or payment.
- Humor should be optional and non-intrusive.

The website should feel alive, not desperate for attention.

---

# 27. PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS

The website must remain fast.

Requirements:

- Lightweight animations
- No excessive parallax
- Lazy-loaded food images
- Optimized imagery
- Skeleton loading states
- Immediate CTA feedback
- Short transitions
- Minimal unnecessary JavaScript

Avoid heavy animation libraries unless absolutely necessary.

Do not sacrifice performance for visual effects.

Use subtle humor where appropriate.

Fast mobile-first UX

Funky.
Fast.
